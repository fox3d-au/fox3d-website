Plant diagnosis

  1. Identify the plant.
  2. Identify the symptoms.
  3. Identify the issue using suitable diagnostic resources.

Investigate positioning/aspect, shade, slope, exposure, coast, microclimates, garden-bed size, plant age, soil type and characteristics, nutrition and irrigation. Consider too little or too much water, root condition/pathogens and hot-weather stress.

Symptoms and disorders

Leaves: chlorosis; old versus new growth; margins, tips, base or interveinal patterns; red colouration; necrosis (tissue death).

Also examine fruit, flower and root development, soil pH, water availability and nutrient toxicities.

Nutrient clues: old versus new leaf discolouration can help distinguish mobile and immobile nutrient deficiencies.

N-P-K: N — vegetative growth. P — roots, fruit and flowers. K — fruit and flowers.

Non-living disorders noted in class: mutation, sunburn, nutrient deficiencies, chemical burn and water stress.

Pests and diseases

Pests: vertebrates, insects, mites, snails/slugs and nematodes.

Diseases: bacteria, viruses, fungi and phytoplasma.

Fungi: multicellular/colonial, heterotrophic, hyphae are filaments, and fruiting bodies produce spores. Bacteria: single-celled, microscopic, reproduce asexually and may live in colonies. Viruses: recorded in class as “not alive”; contain either DNA or RNA and use host cells to reproduce.

Phytophthora

Conditions favouring it

Wet soil, poor drainage, poor horticultural hygiene, susceptible plantings, alkaline pH, calcium-deficient soils and warm soil. Spread can occur through contaminated water and soil.

Control notes

Plant resistant species; mulch; test and adjust soil pH; test soil texture/structure and adjust; remove dead/dying plants; signage. Bush Tucker organic fertiliser and Trichoshield root dip were also noted in class.

Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Class topics included pest review, monitoring and thresholds, biological control and disorder planning. Identify the problem, monitor pest activity and damage, consider thresholds and select suitable control measures rather than automatically relying on chemical control.

Pesticides

Common pesticides noted: oils (Eco Oil/white oil), horticultural soaps (potassium salts of fatty acids), pyrethrum/pyrethrins/pyrethroids, imidacloprid, Success (spinetoram), carbaryl, rotenone and neem oil.

Other materials noted: Mancozeb Plus, wettable sulfur and lime sulfur.

Fungicides noted: Eco-fungicide (potassium bicarbonate), phosphorous acid, liquid copper and copper oxychloride.

Always follow the current registered product label, SDS, workplace procedures and applicable legal requirements. These class notes do not replace the label.

Diagnostic resources

Reference books; reliable websites; image searches as an initial comparison aid; garden centres; Botanic Gardens experts; TAFE SA; universities; SARDI; plant scientists; land management/Landscape SA resources; growers; and PIRSA.
